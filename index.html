The Monday You Already Know

You're teaching the tardigrade unit — one of the best hooks in the curriculum, because the organism itself is genuinely wild. But the five words that explain how it survives are dense, Latin-rooted, and easy to confuse with each other.

You could hand out a worksheet. Make flashcards. Have them copy definitions. It works for the kids who were already going to be fine. Everyone else memorizes just enough to survive Friday's quiz, then loses it by winter break.

Every Vocabulary Word Is Now A Game Mechanic

This isn't trivia bolted onto a game. Each term is the literal solution to a specific, repeated threat. Get the word wrong — or don't react fast enough — and the consequence is immediate and visible, not a red mark three days later.

❄️ Cryptobiosis

A reversible state where metabolism drops to nearly undetectable levels under extreme conditions.
In-game: A drifting Cold Zone and its Frost Mite guardian only back off when this power is switched on. Students feel the difference between "knowing the word" and "knowing when to use it."

🔥 Desiccation

The process of removing nearly all water from a cell or organism.
In-game: Countered in the Heat Zone against the Ember Worm. Reinforces that this is a process, distinct from the resulting shape (Tun State) — a mix-up that shows up on every quiz.

💧 Tun State

The dried, curled-up shape a tardigrade forms when entering cryptobiosis.
In-game: The answer to the Drought Zone and Dust Rotifer. Separating this from Desiccation and Cryptobiosis in gameplay is what finally makes the distinction stick.

☢️ Extremophile

An organism that thrives in conditions most life can't survive.
In-game: The category-level term — countered against the Radiation Zone and Rad Beetle, framed as "what makes the whole unit possible" rather than one hazard among five.

🌌 Ametabolic

A state with no detectable metabolic activity — biology, paused.
In-game: The answer to the Vacuum Zone and Void Wisp, and the correct response during whole-map "surge" events — testing recall under real time pressure, not untimed recognition.

Repetition, Without It Feeling Like Repetition

⏱️

90-second rounds

Short enough to replay 3-4 times in a single class period without losing the room. Every replay is another rep on all five terms.

⚠️

World-wide surges

Every 15-20 seconds, a random hazard hits the entire map with a 3-second warning — a built-in, self-repeating flashcard drill that never feels like one.

🧬

Losing still teaches

Every run — win or lose — ends with the full glossary: definition and mechanism for all five terms, with checkmarks for what the student actually used.

💪

Rival tardigrades

Wild tardigrades roam the map with a visible power level. Switching on the correct survival power doesn't just block a hazard — it makes the student's own tardigrade stronger, reinforcing "knowledge = capability" directly.

Ways to Actually Use This in Class

Bell Ringer

5-minute warm-up

One round while you take attendance. Ask two students to explain why they died, out loud, before you start the lesson.

Pre-Quiz Review

The day before the test

Play in pairs. The loss screen's glossary becomes their last study sheet — but one they actually read, because they just needed it.

Sub Day / Station

Runs itself

Self-contained in one HTML file — no login, no account, no setup. Point a browser at it and the instructions are on screen.

Differentiation

Same game, two goals

Struggling students: survive one full round using the glossary as a safety net. Advanced students: chase "Rivals Outpowered" and "Predators Outsmarted" for a real challenge.
